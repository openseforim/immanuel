Immanuel of Rome, Maḥberot

An English translation in progress · מחברות עמנואל הרומי

Immanuel ben Solomon of Rome (about 1261 to about 1335) wrote 28 maḥberot in Hebrew rhymed prose with poems set into it, including the first sonnets in a language other than Italian. No complete translation exists in any language. This site shows the Hebrew and the English side by side, one row for each line.

Method

  • Lines. The Hebrew source marks each rhyme unit of the rhymed prose. Each unit is one row. Each line of verse is one row.
  • Prose. The English is cadenced prose. Rhyme is kept only where English gives it without added words.
  • Poems. The English is literal, line for line. It does not keep the meter or the rhyme.
  • Allusions. Immanuel builds his sentences from biblical and rabbinic phrases. References in gray brackets identify them. They are the translator's identifications and are not yet checked against a concordance or against Yarden's notes.
  • Uncertain renderings have the mark ⚠ and a note at the end of the page.
  • Translator. The draft is by Claude (Anthropic), directed and reviewed by Ezra Brand.

Caution about the content

The Maḥberot are satire of the 14th century. They include erotic material and mockery of women, of physical defects and of dark skin. The translation does not soften the text.
